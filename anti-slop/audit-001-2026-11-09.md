## Numbered findings

### 1. Hero has no scroll-reveal; landing motion is hover-only despite the "ber animasi" request. (R-19, MEDIUM)

Why: the only motion on the page is `transition-colors` on hover states and the chevron rotate on the FAQ. There is no entrance or scroll-reveal anywhere on the landing, so the page reads static, which is exactly the user's complaint ("lebih bersih clean dan ber animasi"). Fixing it means adding one purposeful, non-endless motion treatment (for example a subtle fade-up as sections enter the viewport) and declaring the MOTION dial value, rather than stacking template animations everywhere.

### 2. Em dashes in landing data and visible copy. (R-02, HIGH)

Why: `em dash` is a Hard Gate. It appears in `apps/web/src/app/(home)/page.tsx` (title/meta/OG/Twitter: "StarsTask — Find. Understand. Contribute.", in comments and structured-data inputs) and in the hero H1 and footer tagline as rendered text. The metadata title is user-visible (browser tab, search result), so it is not exempt. Every em dash must become a comma, period, colon, or parentheses.

### 3. Hero eyebrow pill above the H1. (R-09, MEDIUM)

Why: `landing-hero.tsx` renders a pill with "Built for university students" directly above the H1 "Find. Understand. Contribute." The label adds no fact the headline and subheadline do not already carry, and a pill with an icon parked above the headline is the eyebrow-badge tell. The subheadline already says who the product is for, so the pill is redundant and should be cut or folded into copy.

### 4. Generic Sparkles for the eyebrow and hero note. (R-04, MEDIUM)

Why: `Sparkles` is a generic AI glyph, used twice in the hero (eyebrow icon and "Learn from past work" note). It says "AI" not anything about assignments, archives, or students. The other hero icons (BookOpen, CheckCircle2) are at least content-relevant. A non-generic, assignment-relevant mark or no mark at all is the fix.

### 5. Hero floating notes have no animation or purpose. (R-19, LOW)

Why: three note cards sit absolutely positioned over the illustration with soft shadows. They look like floating "feature chips" but carry no motion and no stated reason for floating. Either the float is a hierarchy device (one focal element) or the notes sit statically. As shipped they are neither, so the animation question (finding 1) decides what they become. Related: the soft shadow on all three notes has no written elevation reason (R-12), and `st-float` in `globals.css` would make them loop forever if applied, which R-19 forbids as decorative endless motion.

### 6. How-it-works is a 3-step round-icon template with a full card grid. (R-05, MEDIUM)

Why: three cards with number badges (1, 2, 3), arrow connectors, and identical padding/illustration framing is the "How It Works always 3 steps" tell. Search, read, contribute is plausibly the real process, so three steps can stay, but the steps are presented as three identical cards competing in uniform rhythm instead of as one process. A single horizontal flow (or a list with the real workflow) would read as a process rather than a template.

### 7. Two copy-paste card grids (value cards + subject grid) in the same template shape. (R-14, LOW)

Why: ValueSection (4 cards) and SubjectGrid (8 links) both render icon + title in identical white cards with the same radius, padding, and accent-soft icon tile. Nothing marks the value cards as higher-weight than the subject links, so features and links flatten into the same shape. The subject grid is navigation, the value list is claims; they should not share the same card language (R-14).

### 8. All icons come from one default library (Lucide). (R-04, LOW)

Why: `lucide-react` is the icon source for every element on the page (hero, strip, steps, value, mission, subjects, FAQ). One thin-stroke rounded library for all icons looks like the model's default icon set, not a product decision. The fix is a written choice (for example, a heavier weight or an academic-figure set) with the reason named, not necessarily a library swap.

### 9. FeatureStrip and SiteSection share the same centered-title + card/list rhythm. (R-05, LOW)

Why: five sections in a row (feature strip, value, how, subjects, mission) each follow one centered header + content grid, so sections blur together even though the composition does vary between list vs grid layouts. With RHYTHM 2 the variation exists but is shallow: only the mission section breaks the pattern noticeably (navy panel + two-column mix). One more composition break (for example, the how-it-works flow in finding 6 going asymmetric) would let the page breathe.

### 10. Three simultaneous accent uses in the hero (pill icon, primary button, icon tiles). (R-29, LOW)

Why: the accent `#45639e` on `accent-soft` tiles, the primary button, and the eyebrow icon's tile is three-ish separate accent moments in the first viewport. The CTA is the right key moment; the eyebrow pill (finding 3) and the icon tiles compete with it. Cutting the eyebrow and consolidating the icon tiles is the cheapest fix.

### 11. Footer is a 4-column template with generic headings. (R-05, LOW)

Why: Browse / Learn / Account / Legal with heading-style columns is the template footer shape. The destinations are all real (no dead links), so this is a LOW structural note: a two-column footer (identity + the handful of real links) would match the product's actual link inventory more honestly.

### 12. No saved preference or `DESIGN.md`: direction is inferred from tokens. (R-37, LOW)

Why: there is no `DESIGN.md` and no antislop settings file, so the palette, typeface, and dials come from the shipped CSS tokens and the calm page composition. That is usable, but it is direction-by-inference, and any bolder redesign (new palette, new accent, animation choreography) needs an explicit direction line from the owner before it can be treated as a deliverable instead of a draft.
